Why we started Keystone

Keystone Realty began with a simple frustration: buying a first home felt like walking into a test nobody had studied for. Terms like "PMI," "escrow," and "contingency" were thrown around, and most agents were more focused on closing the deal than on making sure their clients understood it.

We built Keystone for the people who usually get overlooked — recent graduates signing their first lease, young families buying their first home, and new investors trying to figure out whether a rental property actually makes money. Every client gets the same thing: plain-English explanations, the real numbers up front, and an agent who will tell you when a property is not a good deal.

Our team combines licensed agents, a mortgage specialist, and an investment analyst under one roof, so you never have to piece together advice from five different people. And because we charge sellers a flat 1.5% instead of the usual 2.5–3%, more of your money stays where it belongs — in your home.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I really need 20% down to buy a home?

No. FHA loans can start at 3.5% down and many conventional loans at 3–5%. With less than 20% down you'll usually pay PMI until you reach 20% equity.

How much should I budget for closing costs?

Plan for roughly 2–5% of the purchase price. We'll give you an itemized estimate before you make an offer.

Is it better to rent or buy?

It depends on how long you plan to stay. As a rough guide, buying usually makes more sense if you'll stay five years or longer. We'll run both scenarios with your real numbers.

Why is your rental search free?

Landlords pay our fee when we place a tenant, so renters never pay us anything.
